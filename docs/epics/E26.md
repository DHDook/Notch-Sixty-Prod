## Goal

Quiet Zone works when bass reaches the room through a subwoofer or a bass-management crossover.
Acceptance case: the owner's two-channel DAC into a receiver with a pre-out sub (E25 classes D and
E), refused today with "not available with a subwoofer yet" (E4 question 18). The app measures the
path the anti-noise takes, sub included, and designs, limits and arms against it. Live output still
comes only through the per-feature arm and its safeguards.

## What exists today

- **The refusal.** `FeedForwardLiveArming` refuses when `quietZone.subwooferActive`
  (`NotchSixty/State/FeedForwardLiveArming.swift:29`, `:160`, `:292` "not available with a subwoofer
  yet"); the user text is at `NotchSixty/Audio/ActiveQuietZone.swift:345–346`. The fact is
  `bassManagementConfiguration.enabled` (`NotchSixty/Audio/FeedForwardDriveAligner.swift:140`), fed in
  at `ActiveQuietZoneController.swift:143`. E4 S22b publishes no echo set with bass management or a
  bus split (E4.md design 7, S22b3). The tonal live path has no sub refusal.
- **Over-broad today.** The E25 compiler sets `bassManagementEnabled` for mono bass on the pair
  (class C, `SystemArrangementCompiler.swift:138`), so class C, which has no sub, is refused too.
  E25 S8b will make a downstream sub (classes D and E) count as `subwooferActive`.
- **Where anti-noise enters.**
  - Graph: `ANC_INJECT` is at position 26, after `BASS_MGMT` and `STEREO_CROSSOVER` at 13
    (`core/include/n60/graph.h:74`, `:88`, `:99`). Anti-noise on the stereo graph bypasses mono bass
    and goes to the mains full band. The target is any lane (`graph.h:356`).
  - Stereo session with a sub on the same device: the bridge splitter runs after the renderer
    (`N60RealtimeAudioBridge.c:2943`). Sub = LP(0.5·(L+R)) → all-pass → gain → polarity (`:556–595`),
    so anti-noise reaches the sub. The feed-forward drive frame is the pre-split pair (`:2932`).
  - Through a receiver, the receiver splits the two feeds and sums bass to the sub.
  - Class A (a sub on its own output) compiles to the profile session (`SystemArrangementCompiler.swift:187–192`),
    where Quiet Zone does not run (`TwoChannelAncEligibility.swift:20–21`).
- **Measurement.** The leakage sweep plays raw on outputs 0 and 1 (E4.md S22b design 5). The tonal
  design is L/R only (`ActiveQuietZone.swift:398–399`, joint solve `:704–721`; `N60ActiveQuietZone.h:211`).
- **Safety already sub-aware in part.** `ExcursionCapCurve` has a sub curve flat to crossover × 2 and a
  mains curve, and picks the sub at or below the crossover (`NotchSixty/State/ExcursionCap.swift:93–111`).
  A downstream sub has no known crossover. Arm and safeguards: `LiveTreatmentArm.swift:130`,
  `LiveTreatmentSafeguards.swift:10`, howl guard `graph.h:350`, do-no-harm `DoNoHarmVerifier.swift`.
- **Latency.** One `antiNoisePathSeconds` (`QuietZoneFeedForward.swift:81–86`) and one feed latency
  in `TwoChannelAncEligibility.evaluate` (`:14–44`). Neither has a per-band value.

## Design note

1. **One actuator model: the combined path.** Anti-noise enters where it does today, before any split.
   The secondary path and the leakage are measured through the whole chain (app splitter or receiver),
   so the model is the path the anti-noise plays. This works the same for an app split and a receiver,
   and needs no new output. A sub on its own output (class A) uses the sub lane as an explicit
   actuator instead (decision 7).
2. **`AncBassPath`, a pure Swift value** in `NotchSixty/Audio/QuietZone/` (new folder), derived from
   the arrangement and bass-management state:
   - `.none` (class B);
   - `.monoBassOnPair` (class C): not a sub for ANC, because anti-noise bypasses `STEREO_CROSSOVER`
     and the drive frame already holds the recombined bass. S1 pins this with tests on both
     renderers before it narrows the refusal.
   - `.downstream(ReceiverBass)` (classes D and E), where `ReceiverBass` is `.undeclared`,
     `.openPreout` or `.calibratedLFE(crossoverHz)` (design 10);
   - `.appSplitter(snapshot)` (the stereo-session bus splitter);
   - `.subOutput` (class A, S6).

   A fingerprint (splitter fields, or the receiver declaration and arrangement revision) is stored
   with every ANC measurement; a mismatch makes the record stale ("Your bass settings changed.
   Measure Quiet Zone again.")
3. **The record keeps a plain, documented form** (`CombinedAncPathRecord`): source (pre-split feed L
   or R, or the sub lane), destination (seat or reference microphone), band 15–500 Hz, window, rate,
   fingerprint and the impulse response. A later shared room model can absorb it unchanged (owner,
   3 Nov 2026: that model stays parked). E26 absorbs the planned E4 S22c sub leakage sweep.
4. **Rank-one bass.** A sub fed by the sum of L and R makes the 2×2 plant nearly rank one below
   the crossover, so a free joint solve lets L and R fight and can double the sub's drive. The rank-one band
   comes from the measured plant's per-bin condition number, not from a declared crossover, so the
   same rule works through a receiver. Above 20 dB the solution is constrained to common mode (one
   weight driven equally into L and R). Between 10 and 20 dB a Tikhonov term scales with the
   condition number. This applies to the offline solve, the S18 per-tone update and the feed-forward
   FIR design and block FxLMS.
5. **Latency per band.** Each third-octave band, 20–500 Hz, gets an anti-noise delay: the onset of the
   combined path's band-limited impulse response. That delay includes the sub's low-pass group delay,
   any app all-pass and the receiver's processing. Feed-forward runs only in bands where the reference
   lead covers that delay plus the required reserve. Bands that fail are left to the tonal canceller.
   Tonal is predictive, so it has no latency gate; its phase comes from the measured path and online
   tracking. The app's sub-alignment settings stay as the user set them, and changing them makes the
   record stale.
6. **Safety: stricter, never looser.**
   - Excursion through a receiver follows design 10. With `.openPreout` the caps use the app's own
     bass-management crossover. With `.calibratedLFE` they use the declared receiver crossover, and
     the mains' lower limit is that crossover, because a measurement through the receiver includes
     the sub and cannot show it. With `.undeclared`, tonal and feed-forward Turn On are refused.
   - The common-mode drive is counted once for the sub's thermal integrator, with the summing law
     taken from the measured path gain, not assumed.
   - Path-gain watch: online path tracking compares sub-band gain and phase with the record. A
     change over 6 dB or 90° disarms with a new `LiveTreatmentDisarmReason.bassPathChanged`
     ("Your receiver's level or bass settings changed"). Receiver volume is tracked within that
     window.
   - Feed-forward: the leakage stability bound and the echo set use the combined leakage, which
     covers the sub-to-reference feedback path.
   - The howl guard, do-no-harm verifier, engine lease, caps and the four arming conditions of
     2 Nov 2026 are unchanged and apply first.
7. **Class A (sub on its own output).** Quiet Zone does not run on the profile session today. S6
   adds the ANC stages on that session, with one `ANC_INJECT` per actuator lane after `BASS_MGMT`.
   The canceller band-splits its own output, so low bands go to the sub lane. The leakage sweep plays
   on each output after the split (E4 question 15, first option). It is the riskiest slice (open
   question 2).
8. **Authorisation.** Under the flag, the blanket `subwooferActive` refusal is replaced by
   `SubAwareAncReadiness`. It needs a fresh record with a matching fingerprint, a well-conditioned or
   constrained design, passing safety checks and per-band eligibility. Otherwise it refuses with the
   first failing reason. Readiness is a precondition only: an armed state still needs the user's Turn
   On through `LiveTreatmentArm`, and nothing arms on launch, wake, device or rate change.
9. **Portability and realtime.** All new maths is pure Swift on the control plane, or C++ in
   `core/` with no Apple framework (S6 only). S1–S5 add no realtime code. S6 states the section 4
   contract in its PR.
10. **Receiver bass setups (owner, 4 Nov 2026).** The user declares one of two setups:
    - **(a) Open preout**, the owner's own setup. The receiver's sub preout adds no crossover or
      timing of its own, so the app's bass management is the only crossover and the path is known.
      S2 checks the declaration against the measurement. If the measured path shows an extra
      low-pass or delay that the app's settings do not explain (more than 3 dB or one fd sample),
      Turn On is refused with "Your receiver seems to add its own crossover. Choose Calibrated LFE
      out, or turn its crossover off."
    - **(b) Calibrated LFE out:** the user declares the receiver crossover (default 80 Hz); the
      app measures the combined path.
    - **Undeclared or unknown:** tonal and feed-forward live are refused with "Tell Notch Sixty how
      your receiver handles bass." This is the only new refusal. It is always on, not flag-gated,
      because the caps cannot be trusted without a declaration. It can fire only when an E25
      arrangement records a downstream sub.
    - **Setup note**, shown in plain language beside the declaration: "For the best results with
      Notch Sixty, don't set a crossover in your receiver. Set your speakers to Large and use the LFE
      or sub preout with no receiver crossover. Notch Sixty then handles the bass split."

## Feature flag

`e26.sub-aware-anc` ("Sub-aware Quiet Zone", E26), off by default. Off: the `subwooferActive`
refusal and the S22b echo rule are exactly as today. The one exception is the S1 refusal for an
undeclared receiver, together with its declaration picker, which is always on (design 10). On: bass-path records, per-band eligibility and
`SubAwareAncReadiness` replace the refusal. Only Swift reads it. Live output still needs
`e4.feedforward-live` or `e4.adaptive-tonal-live`, plus the user's Turn On.

## Bench checks (all deferred to E22)

- Owner's system, through the receiver: secondary path, leakage, per-band delays and the
  common-mode readout match the twin's predictions within 1 dB and one fd sample.
- Tonal on a 30–60 Hz tone through the sub: reduction at the seat; no increase at two other seats.
- Feed-forward with music playing: echo removal of at least 20 dB in 31.5–125 Hz with the sub on.
- Receiver trim +6 dB, polarity flip and a crossover change each disarm with `bassPathChanged`.
- Owner's open-preout setup passes the design 10 check; switching on a receiver crossover is refused.
- 25 Hz tone at the cap on the sub, near-field: no distortion or port noise; 10 minutes engages the
  thermal hold.
- Class A on a multi-output interface (S6), at 48 kHz and at the interface's maximum rate.

## Open questions for the owner

Builders follow each default unless the owner answers otherwise.

1. **Home.** Default: a new epic E26, because E4.md is already 1,600 lines. It runs after the E4
   roadmap (Execution order item 1) and before E12 S7.
2. **Class A in v1.** Should Quiet Zone with a sub on its own output (S6) be in v1? Default: yes, last,
   after S5. The owner's own system needs only S1–S5.
3. **Combined path, not a separate sub actuator, on two channels.** Default: yes (design 1). Through a
   receiver it is the only choice.
4. **Tonal live through a receiver.** Answered by the owner, 4 Nov 2026 (design 10): there are two
   declared setups, open preout and calibrated LFE out. Tonal is refused only while the receiver's
   bass management is undeclared or unknown, never just because no crossover is declared. The plan
   recommends making only the undeclared refusal always on, not behind the flag. Everything else
   stays behind `e26.sub-aware-anc`. To confirm: always on for that one case?
5. **Receiver crossover.** Default: entered by the user, defaulting to 80 Hz, and only for
   calibrated LFE out (setup b). Open preout needs no entry. Estimating the crossover from the
   measurement stays post-v1.
6. **Disarm thresholds.** Default: a 6 dB or 90° change in the sub-band path disarms; changes within
   that window are tracked.
7. **Class C.** Default: mono bass on the pair stops counting as a sub for Quiet Zone once S1's tests
   pin it. Today it is refused.
