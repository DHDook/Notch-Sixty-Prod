## Goal

Every shipped feature behaves the same at every device rate from 44.1 to 384 kHz: no capacity,
asset or plug-in limit that only bites at high rates, filter state accurate at 384 kHz, and one
end-to-end rate matrix through production wiring that proves it at the eight standard rates.
CPU headroom is E20's job (Q1).

## What exists today

- **Graph limits are already time-safe at the top.** Max block 2^20 frames, rate to 768 kHz,
  delay to 2^22 frames (10.9 s at 384 kHz) (`core/include/n60/graph.h:52`, `:54`, `:511`). Swift
  derives every DELAY capacity from milliseconds at the build rate
  (`UnifiedGraphConfiguration.delayFrames`, `UnifiedGraphConfiguration.swift`).
- **Frame caps that still bite at high rates (graph path):**
  - Headphone delay: model allows 0–20 ms (`HeadphoneDeviceProfileConfiguration.swift:84`) but
    validation and the translator cap it at 4,095 frames (`:88-91`,
    `UnifiedGraphTranslator.swift:790`, `:829-832`, via `N60HeadphoneDSP.h:19-20`), 10.7 ms at
    384 kHz. A 20 ms profile is refused at 352.8 and 384 kHz. The UI slider stops at 10 ms
    (`ProductionHeadphoneWorkspace.swift:1000`).
  - Fractional delay: FRAC_DELAY has a fixed 8,192-frame ring (`core/src/stages_system.cpp:357`,
    `graph.h:332-336`); a stereo or lane fractional delay of 8,190 frames or more is refused
    (`UnifiedGraphProgramDSP.swift:406`, `UnifiedGraphConfiguration.swift:528`): 186 ms at
    44.1 kHz, 21 ms at 384 kHz. The calibration path already splits whole frames into DELAY
    (`UnifiedGraphConfiguration.swift:515-527`).
  - Fixed 65,536-frame rings and blocks: analyser taps (`UnifiedGraphConfiguration.swift:167`),
    session `maxBlockFrames` (`:190`), N-channel and binaural bridges
    (`CoreAudioNChannelTransportSession.swift:383-385`,
    `CoreAudioBinauralHeadphoneTransportSession.swift:49`), analysis capture
    (`N60RealtimeAudioBridge.h:32`). Each holds 171 ms at 384 kHz; none has a test tying it to
    the largest HAL buffer (`OutputBufferSizeController.swift:87`) or transform it must hold.
  - Protection look-ahead: `laHigh` must be at most 65,536 oversampled frames
    (`stages_system.cpp:259`, `:740`); the UI maximum of 20 ms at 4x and 384 kHz is 30,720, so it
    fits, unproven by a test.
- **Owned elsewhere:** program FIR 32,768 taps (E9 S3, `e9.long-fir`; `graph.h:493-494`);
  binaural 8,192 taps (E13 design note 9; `graph.h:489`); MIMO 4,096 taps (E19 design note 5,
  `N60_STAGE_MIMO` multirate; `N60MIMOFIRRuntime.h:18`); legacy-kernel caps (`N60ProgramLaneEngine.h:19`,
  `N60MultichannelBassManagement.h:20`, `N60FractionalDelay.h:14`, `N60Convolution.c:13`) go with
  the kernel in E23.
- **Precision.** On the graph, PEQ (all user, lane, sub and calibration EQ) keeps double
  coefficients and state (`core/src/snapshot.cpp:57-69`); so do headphone sections, bass
  management (`stages_system.cpp:342`), cleanup (`stages_cleanup.cpp:78`) and the widener
  crossover (E6 G36). Float state remains in loudness (`stages_loudness.cpp:104-108`), multiband
  (`stages_multiband.cpp:71-74`), image apart from the crossover (`stages_image.cpp:82-87`),
  FRAC_DELAY all-pass history (`stages_system.cpp:358-362`) and dynamics envelopes
  (`stages_dsp.cpp:303-305`). E23 S1 measured float biquad state at -81 dB (44.1 kHz) to -51 dB
  (384 kHz) against double; E23 S1b moves the K-weight high-pass, measurement pole, multiband
  crossover and sidechain high-pass to double, and the Type 9 Linkwitz parameters (E6 G18, G25,
  G31). The float legacy biquads (`N60Biquad.h:34-40`) die with the kernel.
- **Assets and rates.** HRIRs are resampled on activation (Swift Kaiser sinc,
  `BinauralProfileAsset.swift:109-175`; cache `BinauralResampledAssetCache.swift:51`; call
  `AudioIOEngine.swift:5591`). Room correction is redesigned at the device rate
  (`AudioIOEngine.swift:4372-4390`). A speaker IR with a stored rate is refused on mismatch
  (`AudioIOEngine.swift:731-738`). SOFA in the research workspace is refused
  (`ProductionOptionalAudioResearchWorkspace.swift:83`); the ANC echo feed resamples its own
  (`FeedForwardLiveEchoFeed.swift:102-109`). Program fold and measurement aggregation refuse on
  purpose (`ProgramFoldDown.swift:168`, `RoomCorrectionProjectController.swift:63`).
- **Audio Units.** The rack accepts rates to 384 kHz and scales the slice to 32,768 frames
  (`AudioUnitHostFoundation.swift:93`, `:111-117`). A plug-in that refuses the rate throws in
  `setFormat` or `allocateRenderResources` (`AudioUnitLiveRackRuntime.swift:552-577`) and is
  quarantined as a generic `renderResourceFailure` (`AudioUnitHostController.swift:1006-1008`,
  reasons `AudioUnitHostFoundation.swift:256-267`): the user is not told the rate is the cause,
  and the quarantine outlives a change back to a supported rate.
- **Rate coverage in tests.** 163 test files exercise 352.8 kHz; 49 declare their own rate list;
  production has `AudioOutputDevice.standardSampleRates` (`RoomCorrectionLaneInstall.swift:140`).
  The PR117 lab (`ci/acoustic_reference_lab/pr117_*`) probes legacy headers and no workflow runs it.
- **Other rate caps.** The ANC feed-forward deadline bridge refuses above 192 kHz
  (`N60FeedForwardDeadlineBridge.c:39`); not tracked in E4 (Q2). Above 384 kHz the graph accepts
  rates to 768 kHz while protection and the AU rack stop at 384 kHz (Q6).
- **CPU.** E20 S1 (#448) measured R3 (7.1.4 + 4 subs) at 90% mean single-thread load at 384 kHz
  on an M4; the rebuild CPU refusal exists but is off (`graph.h:631-637`,
  `UnifiedGraphHost.swift:186`).

## Design note

1. **Rule for capacities.** A capacity is a time. Swift and the core derive frames as
   `ceil(seconds × rate)` at build time; a fixed frame constant is allowed only when a test proves
   it holds the 384 kHz worst case of the user-facing maximum it serves. One helper each side
   (`RateCapacity` in Swift, `n60::framesFor` in the core); no realtime code changes size at run
   time.
2. **Headphone delay.** On the graph the DELAY stage takes `ceil(0.020 × rate)` capacity, so the
   model's 0–20 ms holds at every rate; validation checks milliseconds, not frames. The UI slider
   opens to 20 ms. The legacy kernel keeps 4,096 frames and refuses as today until E23.
3. **Fractional delay.** Any fractional delay splits like the calibration path: whole frames above
   16 go to DELAY, the rest to FRAC_DELAY, so its ring never limits time. No core ABI change.
4. **Proven fixed rings.** The 65,536-frame rings stay; S1 adds a test per ring against the
   largest HAL buffer the buffer controller can request and the largest transform at 384 kHz.
5. **Precision bound.** Every recursive filter and smoother in a graph stage, at its lowest UI
   corner and longest UI time constant, meets the Q4 bound against a double reference at the eight
   rates. E23 S1b fixes its four stages; E7 S4 adds the audit test and fixes any other stage that
   fails. SIMD (E20) never lowers state precision.
6. **Asset resampling.** One control-plane resampler in the core, `n60/resample.h` (Kaiser sinc,
   double, band-limited at 0.95 × the lower Nyquist, amplitude scaled to keep the frequency
   response), replaces the Swift copy in `BinauralProfileAsset`. Assets that carry a rate (speaker
   IR, research SOFA) are resampled when activated at another rate, cached per (asset, rate), the
   original kept; the row shows "resampled from 48 kHz". Measurements used for design or
   verification still refuse a mismatch (re-measure). Never on the realtime thread.
7. **Audio Unit rates.** A rate refusal becomes a per-rate condition, not a quarantine: the slot is
   bypassed at that rate with its bypass latency kept, the rack row says "not available at
   384 kHz", and the slot returns when the rate changes to one it accepts.
8. **Rate matrix.** One suite drives each feature family from its UI model through production
   wiring at the eight rates and checks attach (no fallback), latency report against a measured
   impulse, response against the design, and no refusal outside a named list.

## Feature flag

`e7.rate-adapt` gates S2's resampling of speaker IR and SOFA and S3's per-rate plug-in bypass,
both user-visible changes in what plays. S1 and S4 lift refusals and raise precision with no new
behaviour, proven by tests, and ship without a flag. Only Swift reads the flag. S5 graduates it (default on); the FC phase removes it.

## Owner decisions (10 Nov 2026)

All eight recommendations accepted. Q2 amended: the feed-forward 192 kHz cap has no recorded justification in code or docs. The real limits to check are the bridge's 4,096-frame buffer (10.7 ms at 384 kHz, against 21.3 ms at 192 kHz) and the deadline budget. E4 owns the item "make feed-forward ANC work at 384 kHz, or document the physical reason it cannot" (E4 G8). Live ANC stays inside its authorisation path.

The questions as asked:

1. **Scope split.** E7 keeps capacities, assets, AU rates, precision audit and the rate matrix.
   Moved: program FIR (E9 S3), binaural taps (E13), MIMO taps (E19), the four E23 S1b
   stages (E23), CPU headroom (E20 budgets, E22 bench). Pros: no work done twice; E7 stays
   small. Cons: E7's "every feature passes" depends on other epics shipping. Recommendation:
   approve; E7 S5 checks those items are done or names their owner.
2. **ANC feed-forward 192 kHz cap** (`N60FeedForwardDeadlineBridge.c:39`). E4 raises it with
   tests in the feed-forward live slice, or E7 does it. Pros of E4: ANC code stays under E4's
   authorisation path and reviewer. Cons: E4 has many open slices, so it may wait. Recommendation:
   E4; add an unchecked E4 item in the next E4 PR.
3. **Imported assets.** Resample speaker IR and SOFA on activation (design note 6), or refuse and
   ask the user to re-measure. Pros: works at every rate, as HRIRs already do. Cons: a measured IR
   above its capture Nyquist is band-limited, not measured. Recommendation: resample, with the
   "resampled from" note; measurements keep refusing.
4. **Precision bound.** Error against a double reference at most -100 dB relative to output for
   filters, and at most 1% time-constant error (no stall) for smoothers, at the UI extremes at the
   eight rates. Pros: well below audibility, above float I/O (-81 dB was float I/O limited in
   G36, so the measure is state error, double I/O). Cons: may move more stages to double (CPU).
   Recommendation: yes.
5. **Plug-in refusal.** Per-rate bypass with a visible note (design note 7), or today's quarantine
   with a clearer message. Pros: audio keeps playing and the plug-in returns by itself. Cons:
   the chain sounds different at that rate. Recommendation: per-rate bypass.
6. **Above 384 kHz.** Devices at 705.6 and 768 kHz exist. Refuse with a named reason, or run
   untested. Pros of refusing: matches the tested scope and the protection and AU limits. Cons:
   such a DAC must be set to 384 kHz by the user. Recommendation: refuse, clearly, for v1.
7. **PR117 lab.** Port the applicable cases into the rate matrix and delete the unrun Python and C
   probes, or keep them. Pros: one lab, in XCTest, on the graph; nothing probes headers E23
   deletes. Cons: loses an independent Python reference. Recommendation: port and delete.
8. **CPU before E20.** Until E20's parallel and multirate slices land, R3 at 384 kHz may overload
   an M1. Turn on the rebuild CPU refusal with a named reason now, or leave it to E20.
   Pros of now: a refusal instead of dropouts. Cons: E20 S5 replaces the cost model.
   Recommendation: leave it to E20 (its G4); E7 does not add a CPU gate.
