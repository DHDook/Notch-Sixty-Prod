## Goal

Bring-your-own headphone data is first-class: a user drops in an AutoEq CSV, a REW or squig.link
frequency-response text file, or a target curve, and gets a fitted, applied headphone PEQ in one
click. The same imported targets are usable by room correction. Headphone PEQ and crossfeed are
tuned live while playing with no rebuild. Virtual speakers accept long room-included BRIRs at every
rate to 384 kHz, interpolate HRTFs smoothly under head tracking, and can be captured personally with
in-ear microphones. No third-party measurement or target data ships in the app.

## What exists today

- **Formats**: `MeasurementFormats.parse` (`NotchSixty/Audio/MeasurementFormats.swift:54, :67`)
  reads REW text, FRD and ZMA (tolerant separators, comment lines, 1 MB limit). Commas are always
  separators, so decimal-comma files fail; there is no header-row or named-column support, and no
  AutoEq or squig.link handling anywhere.
- **Target library**: `TargetRecord` schema 1 (`NotchSixty/Audio/RoomCorrectionTargetShaping.swift:104`)
  and `TargetLibrary` (:152, `Application Support/NotchSixty/Targets/`, built-ins, skip-and-report
  load, `importREW` :278). Curve type `RoomCorrectionTargetCurve` (`ProductProfiles.swift:2355`);
  interpolation holds the end values flat (`RoomCorrectionTargetDesigner.swift:158`).
- **Correction tab**: Calibrate → "Bass and correction" (`ProductionSystemArea.swift:41, :48`)
  renders the Room Correction workspace (`ProductionRootView.swift:409`), which mounts
  `RoomCorrectionTargetEditorCard` (`ProductionRoomCorrectionWorkspace.swift:60`). The card has a
  library picker, Duplicate/Save, "Import REW..." (`RoomCorrectionTargetEditorCard.swift:33, :62`)
  and writes the choice through `RoomCorrectionProjectController.setTarget` (:448). The guided flow
  offers only the three built-ins (`SystemSetupBassCorrect.swift:171–195`).
- **Headphone profile**: `HeadphoneDeviceProfileConfiguration`
  (`NotchSixty/Audio/Routing/HeadphoneDeviceProfileConfiguration.swift:285`): target enum
  (neutral, diffuse field, custom; stored only, no rendering effect), headroom 0–30 dB, per-ear gain,
  polarity, delay and up to 16 PEQ bands (±18 dB, Q 0.2–12), crossfeed preset/amount/angle/radius,
  spatial mode, binaural asset, head tracking.
- **Fitter**: `HeadphoneModelCorrectionDesigner.fit` (`HeadphoneModelResearchLibrary.swift:73–190`):
  deterministic greedy peaking-only search, 8 bands, Q ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}, +6/−12 dB, on the production
  biquad response; input `HeadphoneModelMeasurement` (16–256 points carrying L, R and target
  together, mandatory attribution and licence). Output is review-only (`canApply == false`). The
  library imports JSON only (128 KB) and stores per sample rate (:194–224). UI:
  `ProductionHeadphoneWorkspace.swift:294` (import), :324 (fit), :395, staging :619, Apply :872.
  No fitter tests.
- **Live edits**: `HeadphoneLiveEditPlanner` (`HeadphoneLiveEditPlanner.swift:18`) republishes the
  graph on Apply for same-kind changes (E6 S9c/S9d). The HEADPHONE stage (`core/include/n60/graph.h:329`;
  `core/src/stages_system.cpp:293`) runs double-precision sections, but only crossfeed amount and
  ear gains are live; sections, angle, radius and low-pass need a snapshot (rebuild hold ramp).
- **Binaural**: matrix stage, uniformly partitioned (128/256), cap 8,192 taps
  (`graph.h:358–384`; `N60BinauralRenderer.h:19`). Head tracking blends neighbouring yaw sets in
  the frequency domain (`core/src/stages_matrix.cpp:40, :114`); the translator builds 24 sets at
  15° (`UnifiedGraphTranslator.swift:385–387, :465`). Pitch and roll are stored but not rendered.
  Assets are resampled on activation and refused above 8,192 taps
  (`BinauralProfileAsset.swift:58, :80, :96`), so a 48 kHz response longer than 1,024 taps fails

- **Capture**: E11 `MultiMicMeasurementRunner` (`MeasurementSeatPlan.swift:93, :139`) runs K
  shared-clock inputs with a sweep on a chosen output channel. Head tracking source:
  `AppleHeadphoneMotionSource` (`HeadTrackingController.swift:106`). SOFA:
  `SOFABinauralAssetAdapter` (`NativeSOFAImporter.swift:472`).
- **Tests**: `HeadphoneCompletionTests`, `LiveHeadphoneProfileTests`,
  `UnifiedGraphStereoHeadphoneTests`, `UnifiedGraphBinauralTests`, `MeasurementFormatsTests`,

## Design note

1. **One importer, two consumers.** New `FrequencyResponseImporter` (Swift, off the main actor)
   returns `ImportedCurve` (frequencies, dB, optional phase, detected format, source file name,
   issues). Detection: a header row whose first field is `frequency` (case-insensitive) is an
   AutoEq CSV; otherwise REW/squig.link/FRD text through the existing tolerant parser. Columns are
   chosen by name: response = `raw` (else the first numeric column after frequency); an AutoEq
   results CSV that also has a `target` column offers it as a target. **Decimal comma**: when a data
   line holds `;` or a tab, commas inside fields are decimal points. **L/R pairs**: two files whose
   names differ only by a trailing `L`/`R` (or `left`/`right`) become one stereo response; one file
   is used for both ears. Implemented from the public format descriptions; no third-party code,
   data or fixtures (test fixtures are written in the test).
2. **Grid and level.** Curves with more than 512 points are reduced to a 1/48-octave log grid
   (20 Hz–20 kHz, log-frequency box average; deterministic). Targets are normalised to 0 dB at
   1 kHz on import. Responses keep their level; the fitter aligns target to response by the mean
   difference over 250 Hz–2.5 kHz.
3. **One target library.** `TargetRecord` schema 2 adds `category` (`room`, `headphone`, `general`)
   and `provenance` (file name, format, import date, optional attribution). Schema-1 files load as
   `room` and are rewritten only when saved. Headphone and room pickers show their own category plus
   `general`, with a "Show all" toggle; picking across categories shows a one-line caution.
4. **How `RoomCorrectionTargetEditorCard` consumes imported targets** (S1). "Import REW..." becomes
   "Import target..." (flag on): file importer accepts `.txt`, `.csv`, `.frd`; the importer builds
   an `ImportedCurve` → `TargetRecord(category: .room or as chosen in a small sheet)` → validated
   and saved in `TargetLibrary` → the card sets `target = record.curve`, which calls
   `projects.setTarget` exactly as a library pick does today (selected design invalidated, deployed
   playback untouched). Targets imported from the Headphones workspace appear in the same library on
   the next `loadAll()`. Curtains and per-channel offsets apply on top unchanged. The guided flow's
   correction step lists saved `room`/`general` targets after the built-ins. Flag off: the card is
   byte-for-byte today's.
5. **BYO headphone responses** are stored rate-independently in
   `Application Support/NotchSixty/HeadphoneResponses/` (`HeadphoneResponseRecord`: L and R curves,
   source name defaulting to the file name, optional attribution and licence). At fit time the record
   and the chosen target are resampled onto a 1/24-octave grid (20 Hz to min(20 kHz, 0.45 fs), at most
   240 points) to form the existing `HeadphoneModelMeasurement`, with attribution "User-supplied
   file" and licence "Local use" when blank. The old JSON import keeps working.
6. **Fitter: same designer, extended.** Fit range 20 Hz–10 kHz by default (adjustable to 16 kHz;
   zero weight above); low-shelf (105 Hz) and high-shelf (10 kHz) candidates at Q 0.7 alongside
   peaking; default 10 bands per ear (max 16); a deterministic refinement pass after the greedy
   search (frequency × 2^(±1/12), Q from {0.5, 0.7, 1, 1.4, 2, 2.8, 4}, gain in 0.25 dB steps, three
   sweeps, accept only on RMSE drop); boosts ≤ +6 dB, ≤ +3 dB above 10 kHz. Runs detached; ≤ 2 s.
7. **One click.** "Fit and apply" fits, stages the bands, sets headroom to the conservative required
   value rounded up to 0.5 dB, and applies through the existing validated profile path; one level of
   Undo restores the previous profile. The applied profile records the target ID and name
   (`targetCurve = .custom`). This replaces the review-only rule (open question 1).
8. **Live tuning** (S3). Every HEADPHONE parameter becomes live: sections crossfade coefficients
   over the ramp window exactly as the PEQ stage does (type, Hz, dB, Q); angle, radius and low-pass
   ramp. When tuning is on, the snapshot always allocates 16 sections per ear, unused ones at unity,
   so adding or removing a band is a parameter edit. Swift's `HeadphoneLiveTuner` (main actor)
   coalesces draft edits to one mailbox post per 20 ms; a live edit whose positive gain exceeds the
   headroom raises headroom first. Apply saves; Revert and leaving the workspace restore the saved
   profile live. Only spatial-mode, device or delay changes still reconfigure. The engine reads no
   flag; Swift decides.
9. **Long BRIRs** (S4). Responses are split at a rate-independent point, 20 ms after the common
   onset (onset trimmed into the declared latency): the early part stays in the per-orientation
   binaural stage (≤ 7,680 taps at 384 kHz, inside today's 8,192); the late tail, shared across
   orientations, runs in a new `BINAURAL_TAIL` stage with non-uniform partitions (128 → 1,024 →
   8,192), multirate at 176.4–384 kHz (internal rate ≤ 96 kHz, reusing E10's two-band decimator).
   Tail cap 1.0 s; asset limits move from taps to seconds. Graph ABI bump. E13 takes the "binaural
   8,192 taps" cap item from E7; E7 keeps program FIR and headphone delay.
10. **HRTF interpolation** (S5). The translator stores onset-aligned (minimum-phase-like) responses
    plus a per-set, per-source, per-ear delay; the stage interpolates delays with a fractional delay
    line and blends aligned spectra, removing the comb filtering of blending responses with
    different ITDs. Elevation sets (−30°, 0°, +30°, +60°) are blended bilinearly with yaw when the
    asset has elevations and the budget allows; otherwise the translator coarsens the grid
    (15° → 30° yaw, then yaw only) and reports it. Roll stays unrendered.
11. **BRIR capture** (S6–S7). Asset schema 2 adds per-measurement `channelRole` and
    `headYawDegrees` so a BRIR is tied to its speaker; `prepare` selects by role and yaw (non-wrapped
    sets). Capture uses the E11 runner with two shared-clock inputs (left and right ear), a sweep per
    speaker at 1, 3 (default: −30°, 0°, +30°) or 5 head yaws, plus a headphone compensation step
    (headphones on, sweep to the headphone output, five reseats averaged, regularised
    minimum-phase inverse, ≤ 12 dB boost) convolved into the BRIRs.
12. **References**: RBJ Audio EQ Cookbook (existing biquads); Kistler and Wightman 1992
    (minimum-phase plus delay HRTF model); Gardner 1995 and Wefers 2015 (partitioned convolution);
    Lindau et al. 2012, JAES (BRIR mixing time); Kirkeby and Nelson 1999 (regularised inversion);
    Farina 2000 (log sweep).
