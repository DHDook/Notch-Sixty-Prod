## Goal

Stereo plays on every speaker of a 5.1, 7.1 or 7.1.4 system: a stable centre extracted from the front image,
ambience sent to the surrounds and heights, one strength control, and an automatic pass-through when the
source is already multichannel. Works natively at every rate from 44.1 to 384 kHz. One upmix only (owner,
11 Nov 2026).

## Design note

Outline; S1 opens with the full note (derivation, latency, rate, channel and reset behaviour) as AGENTS.md
sections 4 and 10 require.

- **Method: frequency-domain primary/ambience decomposition.** Per STFT bin, smoothed auto- and cross-spectra of
  L and R give a principal (primary) component with its pan position and a residual (ambience) component
  (Avendano and Jot, "A frequency-domain approach to multichannel upmix", JAES 52(7/8), 2004; Merimaa, Goodwin and
  Jot, "Correlation-based ambience extraction from stereo recordings", AES 123rd Convention, 2007). Citations are
  from memory; S1 checks them. A passive time-domain matrix (sum to centre, difference to surrounds) is rejected:
  poor separation and comb filtering, below the "best-in-class" rule.
- **Where sound goes.** The primary component is re-panned by its pan position across the front speakers present
  (L, C, R, and wides when the layout has them) with pairwise constant-power panning (Pulkki, "Virtual sound
  source positioning using vector base amplitude panning", JAES 45(6), 1997). Ambience goes to the surrounds
  (side, then rear) and heights through fixed, seeded decorrelation filters, one per output, so no two
  speakers carry identical ambience. Heights get ambience only, no primary. The upmixer never writes the LFE
  lane; bass management keeps feeding the sub as it does today. Layout handling is by role, not by index, so
  every standard layout in `n60_layout_id` works; tests name 5.1, 7.1 and 7.1.4 plus 5.1.4 and 9.1.6.
- **Strength (one control, 0–100 %).** Strength scales both the share of the primary moved from the phantom
  centre to the centre speaker and the ambience level sent to surrounds and heights. Total output power per
  bin equals input power at every strength, so the level does not jump. At 0 % the output is the input delayed
  by the stage latency, with every other lane silent. Strength is a live, ramped parameter (smoothed per frame).
- **Rates.** Frame length is fixed in time, not in samples: 1024 × 2^round(log2(rate / 48 kHz)), so 1024 at
  44.1 and 48 kHz up to 8192 at 352.8 and 384 kHz (21–23 ms), hop one quarter of the frame, sqrt-Hann analysis
  and synthesis. Smoothing time constants are in seconds. Bins above 20 kHz stay on the front pair (nothing to
  extract up there), which also keeps high-rate content untouched. No SRC.
- **Latency.** Constant per rate (about one frame, 21–23 ms), reported through the graph's latency sum so delta
  audition and E31's total delay include it. Every lane of the stage, including pass-through lanes, carries the
  same delay, so the front pair, extracted channels and LFE stay aligned.
- **Multichannel pass-through.** The stage watches its non-front-pair input lanes (C, LFE, surrounds, heights).
  When any carries signal above -90 dBFS, it crossfades (50 ms) to delayed pass-through of all lanes within one
  hop; it returns to upmixing only after 2 s with those lanes silent. The latency never changes, so the switch is
  click-free. A readback reports the state for the page ("Source is already multichannel, passing it through").
- **Stage and order.** New `N60_STAGE_UPMIX` (kind 54) at position 2.5: after METER_IN, before GAIN and the
  generator. Everything after it (EQ groups, dynamics, loudness, bass management, room correction, routing,
  protection) sees a native wide programme. Input meters still show the real source. The graph ABI bumps once
  (claim the next number in `docs/ABI_NUMBERS.md` at S2 start).
- **Engine home.** All new code is C++ in `core/`: the decomposition and STFT engine in `core/src/upmix/`, the
  stage in `core/src/stages_upmix.cpp`. FFT through `fft.hpp` only; no Apple framework; no third-party code; no
  GPL. Off-thread design (window, decorrelators, FFT setup) is deterministic; all buffers live in the snapshot
  arena. The realtime path follows the C++ realtime subset (no allocation, locks, exceptions). Host block sizes
  are arbitrary (input and output FIFOs inside the stage). Reset: STFT history and smoothing restart from silence.
- **When Swift adds the stage.** Only Swift reads the flag. The builder adds UPMIX when the flag is on, the
  system's upmix is on, the arrangement is class A, and the program layout has at least one role beyond the
  front pair. Never in stereo, Virtual Speakers or headphone graphs, measurement and calibration graphs, or the
  fold-down path. Turning the upmix on or off is a topology rebuild with the normal fade; strength is live.
- **Interactions.** Quiet Zone and E26 run on stereo layouts only, so they never meet the upmixer; the MIMO stage
  sees upmixed lanes as ordinary programme lanes and its parameters are unchanged. S2 tests that adding UPMIX
  leaves every other stage's parameters bit-identical. No ANC or MIMO code is touched.
- **Settings.** `PlaybackSystemControlState` gains `upmixEnabled` and `upmixStrength` (decoded with defaults, so
  older profiles load unchanged).
- **CPU.** Worst case is 7.1.4 at 384 kHz: one forward FFT pair and up to eleven inverse FFTs of 8192 points per
  hop. S2 adds a render benchmark case to `macos-full`; if it misses the E20 budget, S2 moves the inverse
  transforms onto the E20 worker threads (`core/src/jobs.hpp`) before the flag graduates.

## Bench checks (all deferred to E22)

- Stereo music on a real 5.1 and a 7.1.4 system at 48, 96 and 384 kHz: centre image stable, no audible artefacts
  on transients (castanets, piano) or musical noise on ambience, envelopment from surrounds and heights
- A 5.1 film source through the same system: pass-through engages with no doubled content and no click
- A stereo app on a wide device: only the front pair carries signal before the upmixer (confirms S1's finding)
- Lip sync with video at the added 21–23 ms
- CPU at 384 kHz, 7.1.4, on the slowest supported Mac
- Owner listening acceptance of the default strength

## Open questions for the owner

- **Q1 Default state.** Should the upmix be off on every system until the user turns it on, or on by default for
  wider layouts? Recommendation: off; existing systems keep sounding the same, and the Speakers-tab row and the
  page offer it. **Answered (25 Nov 2026): off.**
- **Q2 Default strength.** Recommendation: 50 %, a clear centre and moderate ambience; E22 listening may move it. **Answered (25 Nov 2026): 50 %.**
- **Q3 Patents.** The method comes from papers of 2004 and 2007. S1 searches Google Patents for active claims on
  the steps it uses (decomposition, re-panning, decorrelation) and records them. Recommendation: S1's code goes
  ahead unless the search finds an active claim on a step we use; then S1 stops for your decision (as E35 did).
  Dolby, DTS and Auro upmixers are not imitated and their names are not used. **Answered (25 Nov 2026): as
  recommended.**
- **Q4 Latency.** The upmix adds a constant 21–23 ms. Recommendation: accept; a shorter frame halves it but
  weakens centre separation. The total delay shows in E31. **Answered (25 Nov 2026): accepted.**
- **Q5 Headphones.** Should stereo be upmixed before the Virtual Speakers renderer on headphones? Recommendation:
  no in v1 (speakers only); add it to V2-9. **Answered (25 Nov 2026): no in v1; V2-9 (the
  `docs/V2_ROADMAP.md` edit rides in S1's PR).**
- **Q6 Pass-through control.** Is the automatic multichannel pass-through enough, with the on/off switch as the
  manual bypass, or do you want a separate "Always upmix" override? Recommendation: automatic only; an override
