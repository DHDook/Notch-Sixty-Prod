## Goal

A new user picks an output, says what speakers they have, and is walked through levels and
polarity, distances, a multi-seat sweep, bass, correction and a post-apply verification to a saved,
verified profile, in one flow and without opening any expert workspace. Speakers, Speaker
Calibration, Measurement Lab and Room Correction become one System area, with the room geometry
and treatment advisor inside it and inside the flow. The flow wraps E10's correction and bass and
E11's measurement suite; it adds no new DSP. The software is done when the flow reaches a verified
calibration end to end against the synthetic reference room at all eight rates. The usability test
with a first-time user runs with hardware in E22.

## Design note

1. **System area shape.** The sidebar's System group becomes one **System** row plus
   **Headphones** and **Active Acoustics** (unchanged). System has a tab bar: **Overview** (status
   of the selected output's calibration and the "Set up my system" entry), **Speakers** (today's
   Speakers view), **Room** (geometry and treatment advisor, moved from Tools), **Measure**
   (Measurement Lab) and **Calibrate** (Speaker Calibration and Room Correction as two sections of
   one page: alignment, then bass and correction, then verification). Each existing workspace is
   reused as the tab's content in S1; deeper merging of duplicated cards (microphone, seats) happens
   in S7. The old `ProductionSection` cases stay as aliases that map to `(.system, tab)` so menu
   commands and stored selections keep working.
2. **Flow model.** `SystemSetupFlow` is a pure Swift value type (no SwiftUI, no engine) with an
   ordered step list and a reducer: `start`, `next`, `back`, `skip`, `fail`, `cancel`, `finish`.
   Steps: Output → Topology → Microphone and level → Room (optional) → Measure seats → Alignment
   (levels, polarity, distances) → Bass → Correction → Verify → Save. Each step declares its
   preconditions and what it produces; `next` is refused with a reason until they hold. Steps are
   driven through a `SystemSetupServices` protocol (measure, design, apply, revert, verify, save),
   implemented by the real controllers and by a simulated service over the reference room for tests.
3. **One measurement pass.** The flow measures once: each physical source at each seat, through the
   existing campaign transport (`MultichannelCalibrationController`), multi-mic when several mics
   are present (E11 S9b), otherwise one seat at a time with the seat picker. The seat records feed
   both the calibration designer (alignment) and a `RoomCorrectionProject` (bass and correction)
   through one adapter, so the user never re-measures for correction. Expert workspaces keep their
   own flows.
4. **Topology without E8.** The Topology step offers 2.0, 2.1, 2.2 and the layouts the current
   routing supports, with crossover frequency and slope from today's `BassManagementConfiguration`.
   It writes only through the existing profile APIs. When E8 lands, E8 replaces the step's
   crossover controls with its own editor; the step's contract (a routing and a bass-management
   configuration) stays.
5. **Safe levels.** The level step plays band-limited pink noise from the E11 generator starting at
   −40 dBFS, ramps only on user request, stops at the existing generator ceiling, and reads the SPL
   meter to set a sweep level near 75 dB(C) at the main seat. The flow never enables live ANC or
   MIMO treatment and never touches Active Acoustics.
6. **Apply, revert, cancel.** At start the flow snapshots the selected `PlaybackSystemState`.
   Alignment, bass and correction are applied as trials; Cancel at any step restores the snapshot
   exactly. Measurement steps require playback stopped (today's rule) and the flow stops and
   restarts playback itself with a visible explanation; verification runs through the live graph.
7. **Verification.** The Verify step always re-measures the main seat after apply and shows
   predicted against measured. For mixed-phase and joint-bass designs it is E10 S8's verifier and
   gates `canDeploy`; failure reverts and returns to Correction with the reason. For minimum-phase
   it is informational. A profile saved without a passing report is labelled "Not verified".
8. **Room in the flow.** Room is an optional step before measuring: dimensions and placement from
   `RoomGeometryPlacement`, with seat and speaker suggestions that pre-fill seat names. After
   verification, a "Room insights" card shows the advisor's findings for problems EQ cannot fix
   (decay from E11 RT60, modal nulls left after correction) and links to the Room tab.
9. **Persistence and resume.** A `SystemSetupSession` (schema 1, JSON, one per playback system in
   Application Support) stores the step, choices and measurement IDs, never audio buffers. Quitting
   resumes at the last completed step; a change of output device or sample rate invalidates the
   measurement steps and says why. Finishing saves the profile through `saveCurrentSystemProfile`
   with the calibration summary and verification report.
10. **Entry points.** A "Set up my system" card on the Dashboard and System Overview when the
    selected output has no verified calibration, and a menu item. No automatic sheet at first launch
    (open question 3). Headphone outputs show a pointer to Headphones instead.
11. **Rates.** The flow is rate-agnostic; it records the rate it measured at and relies on E10's
    per-rate synthesis for other rates. Its end-to-end test runs at all eight standard rates.

## Dashboard and menu bar: S12–S14 (owner, 16 Nov 2026)

A review of the Dashboard against the completed-v1 state found that no epic owns the page, its summary cards are
read-only text, some summaries are wrong for routed systems, and there is no health summary. Owner direction
(16 Nov 2026): **the menu bar and the Dashboard must not duplicate controls; each has its own purpose.**

Surface rule (applies to every later slice in every epic):

- **Menu bar panel = the remote control.** Everything the user changes while listening: master volume and mute,
  balance, output device, profile or preset, the E30 quick controls, seat presets (E10 S11), the device rate picker
  (E30) and the level-matched before/after button.
- **Dashboard = the status page.** What is running, whether it is healthy and what needs attention. It shows
  values and links to the page that changes them. It has no everyday controls. Master volume is menu bar only
  (owner, 16 Nov 2026); the Dashboard shows the level as a number.
- **Toolbar** keeps the Processing switch and Bypass; it belongs to the whole window.
- A control lives in exactly one of the two. When the menu bar item is switched off in Settings, the same panel
  (one SwiftUI component) opens from a toolbar button instead, so the controls are never unreachable and never
  shown in both places at once.

Order: S9 (app-shell split) first, then S12, S13, S14, all before E30 so that the E30 controls land in the remote.
All three sit behind `e12.dashboard-remote`; S14 graduates it. No slice adds work to the audio callback.

### S28b `e12/blind-system-sides`: system-profile sides, room correction and bass in the match, graduate (needs-owner: engine override; owns G79 remainder; owner ruling 22 Nov 2026)

Approach written and approved 26 Nov 2026 (owner accepted Q1–Q4 below). Two PRs, both needs-owner: S28b1 is the
engine override and the level model (realtime-reviewer), S28b2 the page sides and the graduation. About 650 and 550
changed lines of product code.

**What exists.** Sides are content only: `ComparisonCandidates.list` (`NotchSixty/State/BlindLiveComparison.swift:32`)
offers current, processing off (EQ, preamp, dynamics) and content presets, played by `ProfileContentAuditionPlayer`
(`:136`) through `transitionContentAuditionState` (`NotchSixty/Audio/AudioIOEngine+EQAndGainSetters.swift:213`),
which reattaches room correction, bass management and the Playback System unchanged. The level model `levelDB`
(`BlindLiveComparison.swift:74`) is content EQ power plus preamp and headroom. The only system-domain override is
`RoomCorrectionABXOverride` (`NotchSixty/Audio/Routing/RoomCorrectionABXLanes.swift:12`; slot
`AudioIOEngine.swift:1173`, setter `:1194`, applied in `AudioIOEngine+ProgramApply.swift:463`, cleared on stop in
`AudioIOEngine+Transport.swift:730`), which swaps lane installs only. Applying a saved system profile
(`ProductProfiles.swift:2268` `applySystemState`) is a chain of `replace…` calls that write stored configuration and
republish several times, so it cannot be a blind side. The bass-management stage takes crossover Hz and redirect dB
live; its topology is not live (`core/include/n60/graph.h:381`), so a topology change is a faded rebuild.
`n60_headroom_evaluate` (`core/include/n60/headroom.h:102`, `core/src/headroom/headroom.cpp`) already models the
composite per-lane response including crossovers, sub sums, FIR stages and delays, for peak only.

**Design.**
1. *One audition override slot.* `RoomCorrectionABXOverride` becomes a case of one never-persisted
   `AuditionOverride` (new file `NotchSixty/Audio/Routing/AuditionOverride.swift`): `roomCorrection` (`.applied`,
   `.off`, or `.lanes(RoomCorrectionLaneInstall)`), `bassManagement` (`BassManagementConfiguration?`, nil = applied),
   `outputGainDB` (nil = applied), `gain` (linear trim, at most 1) and `latencyPadFrames`. The ABX sheet and the
   page use the same slot, so two overrides can never stack. The setter and the unavailable reason move to
   `AudioIOEngine+AuditionOverride.swift`; `AudioIOEngine.swift` only renames its stored property and gets shorter.
   The graph build (`ProgramApply`, `renderBassManagementConfiguration`) reads the override in place of the stored
   value, so one republish with the normal fade moves every changed domain at once. Stored configuration is never
   written, so a restore is "clear the slot and republish", the same path as today's ABX close.
2. *Engine code stays where it is.* The override is control-plane Swift choosing which plan to publish; no DSP moves
   to Swift and no engine C is replaced. The realtime core is expected to need no change (room correction off and
   bass management off are graphs the builder already makes). If the builder finds a side the core cannot build at
   the same output layout (for example a silent sub lane with the bass stage removed), the fix is a C++ change in
   `core/src` with a graph ABI bump and a C harness, never in `NotchSixty/Audio/Realtime`; that turns S28b1 into a
   realtime PR and it says so.
3. *Latency match.* Room correction off removes FIR latency, which is an audible timing clue and moves lip sync.
   Each side's declared latency is read from the built plan; the shorter side gets `latencyPadFrames` on every
   source lane (mains and subs) before bass management (`N60_STAGE_LANE_DELAY`), as the ABX does. A pad past
   `N60_GRAPH_MAX_DELAY_FRAMES` makes the pair unavailable with a reason; it never plays unmatched.
4. *Which system sides exist.* A saved system profile is a side only when it matches the running system in output
   device, output layout and channel count, subwoofer count, output and speaker routing, speaker-driver processing,
   headphone profile and Playback System controls (upmix, crossfeed, virtual speakers, audition mode). It may differ
   only in room correction, bass management with the same `physicalOutputMode` (main-plus-sub or none), and output
   gain. Others are listed greyed with the first difference in plain words ("uses a different output"). The side
   keeps the current content settings, so it compares systems only. Its room correction is installed at the running
   rate by the same lane-install path the profile apply uses; if that needs a design at this rate, it runs off the
   main thread while the test prepares, never on a switch.
5. *"Everything off" side.* Beside "Processing off (equaliser, preamp, dynamics)", a new side "Everything off"
   turns off room correction and bass management as well. It never turns off output protection, speaker-driver
   processing, a bi-amp or tri-amp splitter, routing or master volume, and the page says so in one line. Turning
   bass management off sends full-range bass to the mains; the guard is Q1.
6. *Level match with room correction and bass.* New core maths, C++ in `core/src/headroom`: a weighted-level entry
   beside `n60_headroom_evaluate` that returns the K-weighted pink-noise power of one output's composite response
   on the same 1/48-octave grid (headroom ABI 3 to 4, reported by `n60_core_abi`). Swift fills the output
   descriptions from the stage descriptors it already publishes for headroom (content EQ, bass management, sub
   gain and polarity, room FIR, output gain) for every output lane of each side and sums lane powers. Content-only
   pairs keep today's model, so existing results do not move. The louder side is trimmed, the remaining difference
   shown, "approximate" kept for active dynamics and added when the model has no measured response (Q2).
7. *Safety and exits.* The override never touches `LiveTreatmentArm`. System and "Everything off" sides are
   unavailable while live Quiet Zone or MIMO room treatment is armed or running (changing correction or bass moves
   the secondary path they were designed on); content sides stay available as today. Arming during a test ends the
   test and restores first. The slot is cleared and the applied configuration restored on: close, leaving the page,
   Stop, output change, nominal-rate change, device loss, sleep, normal Quit (before the stop fade), a protection
   fault, opening the setup ABX sheet, and any change to a system or content setting made elsewhere (menu-bar
   profile switch included). Never persisted, so a crash or force-quit relaunches with none. Both sides are built
   and validated before the first trial; a switch that throws ends the test and restores.
